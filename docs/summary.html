Quartermaster

An AI procurement assistant with human approval. Project 7 prerequisite, built by Gargi Gupta. Live at quartermaster-a2cb.onrender.com.

The problem

A request such as "20 laptops for the new Engineering hires in Pune" takes a procurement team several checks: whether stock or another office can cover it, which vendors are allowed, the cheapest valid price, tax and currency, the department budget, and who must sign off. Each check is a chance to miss a rule. An AI can do this legwork fast, but it must not be trusted to invent figures, skip a rule or place an order on its own.

What it does

  • Purchase requests: works out stock, transfer or purchase, the vendor, the total with GST, and the approvers, with policy reasons and sources.
  • Questions about policies, contracts and vendors, answered with the exact page, row or email cited, or declined if the documents do not cover it.
  • Missing details become questions back to the employee; forbidden items are refused.
  • Nothing is bought until every required approver approves on the page.

How it works

Screen the request→Understand→ Investigate with tools→Draft decision→ Check figures, sources, support→Pause for approvers→ Create order
  • The AI proposes; code decides. Totals, tax, currency, approval levels and vendor rules come from code. The AI never sees the tool that creates orders.
  • Enterprise knowledge: 11 documents in 6 formats (policies, PDF contract, Word memo, email thread, spreadsheet rate card, vendor database) in one search index with exact citations.
  • Hallucination checks: every figure, vendor and citation is checked against the records, and a second AI call confirms each cited passage supports its claim. Anything unsupported goes to a person instead of the employee.
  • Failure handling: a backup AI model, screening of requests that try to instruct the AI, safe loading of broken or tampered documents, and usage limits per visitor and per day.

All 3 remaining misses were caught by the checks and sent to a person; none reached the employee as a wrong answer. The first run exposed six bugs, all fixed. Rerunning the original prompts on 22 cases scored 19/22 against 20/22, so most of the gain came from code, not prompt wording. 266 automated tests run on every push.

Built with, and limits

All free: LangGraph, Groq (gpt-oss-120b, backup gpt-oss-20b), fastembed and Chroma, FastAPI, SQLite, Docker on Render, GitHub Actions. Limits: one product per request; free-tier AI allowance causes waits; the free server sleeps and forgets requests on restart; the company and its data are fictional.

Kestrel Works, its policies, vendors and figures are fictional. No real orders are placed.
